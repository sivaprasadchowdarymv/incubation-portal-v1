# Peer Review Guidelines for Evaluating Abstracts

## Your assignment
The organisers assign each accepted abstract to reviewers. You receive an email with the paper code (for example P-0012) and a due date. Open My Reviews, then accept the assignment or decline it. Decline and tick conflict of interest if you know the team, are their mentor or teacher, work in the same lab or department, or have any reason you cannot judge fairly. Declaring a conflict is always the right choice when in doubt.

## Blind review and confidentiality
Abstracts are reviewed blind: you do not see the team name or members, and the file is named with the paper code. Do not try to identify the authors. Do not share, copy or use the ideas in any abstract. Delete downloaded files after the evaluation is finished.

## How to score
Score every rubric criterion from 1 (poor) to 5 (excellent): relevance to the problem statement, novelty or originality, technical soundness and feasibility of the methodology, impact and scalability, and clarity of deliverables. The weighted total is shown out of 100. Choose an overall recommendation (strong accept, accept, weak accept, weak reject, reject) and your confidence from 1 (guess) to 5 (expert).

## Writing comments
Comments for the team are shared anonymously as Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2. Be specific, respectful and constructive: name the main strengths, the main weaknesses, and concrete suggestions. Confidential comments go only to the organisers; use them for concerns such as suspected plagiarism.

## Deadlines and revisions
Submit before the due date; reminders are sent 48 and 24 hours before, and daily when overdue. Save a draft at any time. After you submit you can read the other reviews of the same abstract and revise yours until the organisers record a decision.
